Spatial encodings

Anatomical region + relative position

Code: towers.py ↗ · pe.py ↗
Exact coordinate convention

The region table has 74 atlas labels plus an unknown row. Contact position is the integer from the clinical label, with gaps preserved—not millimeters or a whole-brain coordinate. Time is on the shared 32 Hz lattice. The released RoPE bases are 8 (contact) and 64 (time). For one frequency, R(p)q · R(r)k = qᵀR(r−p)k. A common coordinate shift preserves this dot product for fixed Q/K content; this is not a claim that the entire model ignores anatomy. Paper: positional encoding and region appendix ↗

Readout

One-second trials · 4 + 16 + 32 = 52 tokens/contact · encoder frozen

Code: encode.py ↗ · readout.py ↗
Evaluation protocol

Neuroprobe trials span 0–1 s after word onset. Keep band and time coordinates; flatten rather than average them. One ridge is fit per binary task. Cross-session intersects contact labels when sessions retain different contacts. Cross-subject averages contacts by atlas region across all arrays, intersects the anchor/target regions (3–7 in the benchmark), and excludes the unknown label. The figure uses the real S1/S10 shafts only to illustrate pooling; the benchmark anchor is subject 2, session 4.

Ridge weights and feature scaling use training trials only. Select λ by validation AUROC from 25 log-spaced multipliers (10⁻⁴–10⁴), scaled by the mean squared training-feature norm; ties choose the smallest λ. Cross-subject validation uses target labels; the separate target test split is used for reporting. Within-session and cross-session evaluate both directions. Report mean AUROC across sessions for each task, then macro AUROC over 15 tasks.

Matched control: frontend → readout versus frontend → frozen encoder → readout. Same trials, splits, preprocessing and ridge recipe; the baseline uses raw clipped/subsampled bins, not learned stem tokens. Only pretraining learns encoder weights; the decoder is discarded for evaluation. Results and spatial ablations on the project page ↗

Masking & loss details

Randomly mask exactly 75% of tokens per shaft/clip; the fraction per contact or band can vary. Average squared error over each token’s valid bins, then over masked tokens. The mid band’s padded seventh bin is excluded. Bands are not equally weighted: they contribute 8 / 32 / 64 tokens per contact. Targets are session-normalized STFT magnitudes, with no extra per-token normalization. No teacher. Decoder attention carries gradients back to visible encoder outputs.

The paper’s run uses 27.9 hours from 7 subjects, 2-second clips, AdamW for 55,000 steps, and a 5k warmup / 45k constant / 5k cooldown schedule. Subjects 7 and 10 are excluded from pretraining; checkpoint timing is chosen on pretraining sessions, not benchmark test scores. Training excerpts are selected from the research implementation; they are not a standalone training package.

Compute choices

Exact-count 75% masking keeps the visible total fixed. Shaft count and lengths still vary, so compilation uses dynamic=True. The final run uses two NVIDIA H100 GPUs with BF16 mixed precision. Counts refer to sampled contact-windows, not unique electrodes. The run sets shaft_alpha=0: subject weights are equal, shafts are drawn uniformly within subject, and clip starts are uniform over valid aligned starts. Artifact-overlapping clips are excluded. The schedule axis counts optimizer updates, not microbatches. In code, warmup uses (step + 1) / 5000; the first update is at 1.2e-6 and the drawn zero is the conceptual starting boundary. The scheduler class is named WarmupCosine, but the run selects decay_shape="linear". stable_steps=50000 is the plateau end, not its duration. The active sampler uses no filler; no separate padding to the longest shaft is needed.
